"Influencers in Toronto" is a big search for a specific problem. Most brands typing it don't want a ranked list of the city's biggest accounts. They want to know: where do I actually find someone the right size for my budget, what should I expect to pay, and how do I tell a real local audience from a bought one before I send money. This is the short version of everything we've learned running a Toronto creator marketplace, with links to the longer version of each piece.

## Start with size, not fame

"Influencer" covers everyone from a 2,000-follower home cook to a national brand ambassador with a talent agent. For most local businesses, the useful range sits at the smaller end: **nano creators** (roughly 1,000 to 10,000 followers) and **micro creators** (roughly 10,000 to 100,000). Both post to audiences that are still mostly real people who chose to follow a person, not a brand, which is the entire reason their recommendation carries weight. Above that tier you're usually paying for reach outside Toronto, which is wasted spend if your business is a single storefront.

If you're not sure which of the two fits your goal, [Micro vs nano creators: which fits a Toronto brand?](https://sixthdegree.app/blog/micro-vs-nano-creators/) walks through the tradeoff with a worked $1,000 budget.

## Where to find them

The obvious searches, "Toronto influencers" and a bare niche hashtag, mostly surface the wrong end of the market: agency roundups and a wall of thumbnails with no way to tell who's local and active. The fix isn't a better keyword. It's searching by place instead of by topic (location tags, neighbourhood hashtags, who's already tagging businesses like yours) and, eventually, a verified directory instead of DM roulette. We wrote up the full method, including exactly which searches to run and how to write an opener that gets a reply, in [How to find micro influencers and UGC creators in Toronto](https://sixthdegree.app/blog/find-micro-influencers-ugc-toronto/). This post picks up from there: once you have a shortlist, here's what it costs and how to vet it.

## What it costs

Ranges we quote when Toronto brands ask, in Canadian dollars:

| Deliverable | Nano | Micro |
|---|---|---|
| Static feed post | $50 to $150 | $250 to $800 |
| Reel / short video | $100 to $250 | $400 to $1,500+ |
| Story set | $30 to $100 | $150 to $500 |

Usage rights change the number: the base rate covers the creator posting to their own account, and running the same content as a paid ad from your brand's account typically adds 25% to 100% on top. The full table, including UGC-only pricing and two worked campaign budgets, is in [Toronto creator rates: what nano and micro cost in 2026](https://sixthdegree.app/blog/toronto-creator-rates/).

## Vetting before you hire

A profile that looks right can still be wrong. Three checks before you send a message:

1. **Read the comments, not the like count.** Likes are easy to inflate. Specific, conversational comments from people who sound like they know the creator are much harder to fake at scale.
2. **Look for local signal in the audience, not just the bio.** A Toronto location tag is one line of text. Neighbourhood mentions and other local accounts showing up in the comments are the real signal.
3. **Scroll back three or four months**, not just the best nine posts. A follower jump with no matching engagement bump is worth a second look.

The longer checklist, plus how to read past brand work in a creator's grid, is in the find-influencers post linked above.

## "Creators near me" and the local advantage

A lot of this search happens as "influencers near me" or "creators near me," which resolves by the searcher's own location rather than a typed city name. There's no content trick that wins that search on its own. What actually helps: staying specific about Toronto and its neighbourhoods rather than writing generically about "influencer marketing," since that's the same local signal Google is trying to match searchers against. If you're a Toronto business with a physical location, claiming and filling out your Google Business Profile is worth doing before you spend a dollar on creators, since local search results pull from it directly.

## Usage rights, decided upfront

Before you hire, decide one thing and say it in your first message: are you only using the content once, for the creator's own post, or do you also want to repost it, run it as a paid ad, or keep using it after the campaign ends? This is the single most common source of after-the-fact disagreement between brands and creators, and naming the answer costs you two lines in a DM.
