Influencer Marketing Agency vs. Marketplace in Toronto

By Rohan Gandotra, co-founder of Sixth Degree · August 31, 2026

Search "influencer marketing Toronto" and the results mix two different services together. Some are agencies pitching a full campaign, strategy, creator casting, content review, a report at the end. Others are marketplaces, ours included, pitching direct access to creators and the deal infrastructure to hire them. They read like the same pitch. They are not, and the difference changes both what you pay and how much of the work lands on your desk.

What an agency does

A Toronto influencer marketing agency runs the campaign for you. You give them a goal and a budget, and they handle strategy, find and negotiate with creators, brief them, review content before it posts, and send you a report when it's done. You are buying labour and judgment, not just access to creators. For a brand with no time and no in-house experience running these campaigns, that's real value.

The tradeoff is cost and distance. Agencies typically charge a management fee on top of what the creators are paid, and most set a minimum monthly retainer or campaign budget to make the arrangement worth their time. That minimum is the part that prices out a lot of small businesses: a single-location café or gym often has a budget that covers a handful of creator posts, not enough to clear an agency's floor. You're also one step removed from the actual creators. The agency picks who's on the list, negotiates the rate, and reads their first-pass content review, so you find out about a mismatch after time and money are already spent.

What a marketplace does

A marketplace hands you the tools and gets out of the way. You browse or get matched with creators, see their rate before you message anyone, negotiate directly, and pay through the platform instead of e-transferring a stranger. You're doing the strategy and the outreach yourself, but there's no management fee stacked on top of the creator's rate, and no minimum spend to clear before anyone will work with you.

This is the model we built Sixth Degree around: a directory of human-verified Toronto creators, visible rates, and payment held in escrow until the work is confirmed live, so you're not wiring money to an Instagram handle and hoping. What each actually costs

Whichever route you pick, the creator's own rate doesn't change much. Toronto creator rates: what nano and micro cost in 2026 has the current ranges by tier and format. What changes is everything stacked on top of that rate. A marketplace platform fee is usually a flat percentage of the deal, charged once, shown before you commit. An agency's management fee is a separate line, typically billed as a percentage of total campaign spend or a flat retainer, and it exists whether the campaign has three creators or thirty. For a single campaign with two or three Toronto creators, the agency fee and minimum often cost more than the creators themselves.

When the agency is the right call

Hire an agency when you genuinely don't have the time to run this yourself, when you're coordinating a national or multi-market campaign with dozens of creators at once, or when you want someone else accountable for strategy and reporting to a board or a client. The fee buys you that accountability. If your team has zero hours to spend on outreach this quarter, paying for someone else's hours is a reasonable trade.

When a marketplace (or doing it yourself) is the right call

Most single-location Toronto businesses, cafés, gyms, boutiques, local service brands, don't need a national program. They need three to eight local creators who actually eat at the restaurant or work out at the gym, posted on a budget that's closer to a thousand dollars than ten thousand. At that scale, an agency's minimum spend and management fee eat a budget that should be going to creators and their work. Influencers in Toronto: how to find and hire them is the fuller guide to finding and vetting that kind of creator directly, including what to look for beyond follower count.

What to check no matter which route you pick

Two things matter regardless of who's running the campaign. First, is the creator actually vetted, real local audience, not a bought following, and not just a big number with no engagement behind it. Second, is usage rights settled in writing before anyone posts: are you only getting the creator's own post, or do you also want to run the content as a paid ad or keep it after the campaign ends. That second question is the most common source of disputes we've seen described, and it costs two lines in a message to settle upfront. What is an ad rights rider? covers exactly what to put in writing and why the default (one organic post, nothing else) surprises brands who assumed otherwise.

An agency should be handling both of these for you as part of the fee. A marketplace should be handling the first one (verification) structurally, and giving you the tools to settle the second one directly with the creator.
